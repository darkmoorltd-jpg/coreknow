# Solubility

## PART 1: What is Solubility?

### 1.1 Simple Definition

Solubility is the MAXIMUM amount of a solute that can dissolve in a given amount of solvent at a specific temperature.

### 1.2 Symbols You Must Know

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|--------|---------|------|
| S | Solubility | g/100g solvent OR mol/dm3 |
| m_solute | Mass of solute | g |
| m_solvent | Mass of solvent | g |
| n | Moles of solute | mol |
| V | Volume of solution | dm3 |
| T | Temperature | C or K |

### 1.3 Key Terms

**Solute:** The substance that dissolves (e.g., salt).
**Solvent:** The substance that does the dissolving (e.g., water).
**Solution:** The homogeneous mixture of solute + solvent.

### 1.4 Units of Solubility

Solubility is commonly expressed in three ways:

1. Grams per 100 g of solvent (most common in JAMB)
   Example: Solubility of KNO3 at 40 C = 65 g/100g water

2. Grams per dm3 (or per 100 cm3) of solution
   Example: Solubility of NaCl = 36 g/100 cm3 water

3. Moles per dm3 (mol/dm3)
   Example: Solubility of CuSO4 = 0.5 mol/dm3

## PART 2: Types of Solutions

### 2.1 Unsaturated Solution

A solution that CAN still dissolve more solute at that temperature.

**Example:** Adding 1 spoon of sugar to a large cup of water.
Most of the sugar dissolves and more can still dissolve.

### 2.2 Saturated Solution

A solution that CANNOT dissolve any more solute at that temperature.
If you add more solute, it just sits at the bottom undissolved.

**Example:** Adding spoon after spoon of sugar to water until no more dissolves.

### 2.3 Supersaturated Solution

A solution that contains MORE solute than a saturated solution at that temperature.
This is UNSTABLE. Adding a seed crystal causes sudden crystallization.

**How it forms:**
1. Make a saturated solution at HIGH temperature.
2. Cool it down SLOWLY without disturbing.
3. The extra solute stays dissolved temporarily.
4. Add a seed crystal and it crystallizes rapidly.

### 2.4 Comparison Table

| Type | More solute dissolves? | Stability |
|------|------------------------|-----------|
| Unsaturated | Yes | Stable |
| Saturated | No | Stable |
| Supersaturated | Yes (temporarily) | UNSTABLE |

## PART 3: Solubility Curves

### 3.1 What is a Solubility Curve?

A solubility curve is a graph showing how the solubility of a substance changes with temperature.

**Axis setup:**
  X-axis = Temperature (usually in C)
  Y-axis = Solubility (usually g/100g water)

### 3.2 Three Patterns

**Pattern 1 — Solubility INCREASES with temperature**
Most solids behave this way.
Examples: KNO3, CuSO4, NaCl (slightly)

**Pattern 2 — Solubility barely changes**
Example: NaCl (only slightly increases)

**Pattern 3 — Solubility DECREASES with temperature**
Some solids and ALL gases behave this way.
Examples: Ce2(SO4)3, Ca(OH)2, all gases

### 3.3 Why Do Gases Decrease with Temperature?

Dissolving a gas in water is EXOTHERMIC (releases heat).
By Le Chatelier's principle, increasing temperature pushes the equilibrium BACK toward the gas (not dissolved).

**Everyday example:** Warm soda goes flat quickly because CO2 escapes faster at higher temperature.

### 3.4 Reading Solubility Curves

**Worked Example 1**

**Question:** The solubility of KNO3 at 40 C is 65 g/100g water. What mass dissolves in 200 g of water at 40 C?

**Solution:**
  Solubility = 65 g per 100 g water
  For 200 g water:
  Mass = (65 / 100) x 200
  Mass = 130 g

**Answer: 130 g of KNO3 dissolves.**

**Worked Example 2 — Crystallization on Cooling**

**Question:** A saturated solution of CuSO4 is made at 60 C (solubility 40 g/100g water). It is cooled to 20 C (solubility 20 g/100g water). How much CuSO4 crystallizes out?

**Solution:**
  At 60 C: 40 g dissolved in 100 g water
  At 20 C: only 20 g can stay dissolved
  Difference = 40 - 20 = 20 g

**Answer: 20 g of CuSO4 crystallizes out.**

**Worked Example 3 — Two Different Masses**

**Question:** 500 g of water is saturated with KNO3 at 40 C (solubility 65 g/100g water). The solution is cooled to 10 C (solubility 20 g/100g water). How much KNO3 crystallizes out?

**Solution:**
  At 40 C: (65/100) x 500 = 325 g dissolved
  At 10 C: (20/100) x 500 = 100 g can stay dissolved
  Crystallized = 325 - 100 = 225 g

**Answer: 225 g of KNO3 crystallizes out.**

## PART 4: True Solutions, Suspensions, and Colloids

### 4.1 The Big Picture

When you mix two substances, you can get three different types of mixtures based on particle size.

### 4.2 True Solution

**Definition:** A homogeneous mixture where the solute particles are completely dissolved and are the size of individual molecules or ions.

**Particle size:** Less than 1 nm

**Properties:**
- Homogeneous (uniform throughout)
- Transparent (clear)
- Does NOT scatter light (no Tyndall effect)
- Particles cannot be separated by filtration
- Particles do not settle on standing
- Particles cannot be seen with a microscope

**Examples:** Salt water, sugar water, vinegar, air

### 4.3 Suspension

**Definition:** A heterogeneous mixture where relatively large solid particles are suspended in a liquid.

**Particle size:** Greater than 1000 nm

**Properties:**
- Heterogeneous
- Opaque (not clear)
- Scatters light (Tyndall effect)
- Particles can be separated by filtration
- Particles SETTLE on standing
- Particles visible to naked eye

**Examples:** Muddy water, sand in water, chalk in water, Harmattan haze, water paints

### 4.4 Colloid

**Definition:** A mixture where particles are intermediate in size — larger than in a true solution but smaller than in a suspension.

**Particle size:** 1 to 1000 nm

**Properties:**
- Appears homogeneous but is actually heterogeneous
- Often cloudy or milky
- Scatters light (Tyndall effect)
- Cannot be separated by ordinary filtration
- Does NOT settle on standing
- Particles visible with a microscope

**Examples:** Milk, blood, fog, aerosol spray, emulsion paints, rubber solution, mayonnaise, smoke, jelly

### 4.5 Comparison Table — Memorize This!

| Property | True Solution | Colloid | Suspension |
|----------|---------------|---------|------------|
| Particle size | < 1 nm | 1 - 1000 nm | > 1000 nm |
| Appearance | Clear | Cloudy | Opaque |
| Homogeneous? | Yes | Intermediate | No |
| Tyndall effect | No | YES | YES |
| Settles on standing? | No | No | YES |
| Filterable? | No | No | YES |
| Example | Salt water | Milk | Muddy water |

### 4.6 The Tyndall Effect

**Definition:** The scattering of light by particles in a colloid or suspension.

**Test:**
- Pass a beam of light through a sample.
- TRUE SOLUTION: light passes straight through, no visible beam.
- COLLOID or SUSPENSION: you see the beam scattering (like car headlights in fog).

## PART 5: Solvents and 'Like Dissolves Like'

### 5.1 The Rule

POLAR solvents dissolve POLAR solutes.
NON-POLAR solvents dissolve NON-POLAR solutes.

This is called 'Like Dissolves Like'.

### 5.2 Polar vs Non-Polar

**Polar molecules:**
- Have partial positive and negative ends
- Examples: Water (H2O), ethanol, ammonia, HCl

**Non-polar molecules:**
- No charge separation
- Examples: Petrol, benzene, oil, grease, paraffin

### 5.3 Common Solvents and Their Uses

| Solvent | Type | Dissolves |
|---------|------|-----------|
| Water | Polar | Salts, sugars, acids |
| Ethanol | Polar | Fats, oils, perfumes, dyes |
| Petrol | Non-polar | Grease, paints, oil stains |
| Benzene | Non-polar | Rubber, plastics, dry-cleaning |
| Acetone | Polar | Nail polish, glue, resins |
| Kerosene | Non-polar | Wax, oils, pesticides |

### 5.4 Why Certain Solvents Are Used for Stains

**Removing oil stains from clothes:**
- Oil is non-polar.
- Water is polar → does NOT remove oil.
- Petrol/kerosene is non-polar → removes oil.

**Removing salt stains from clothes:**
- Salt is polar (ionic).
- Water is polar → removes salt easily.
- Petrol is non-polar → does NOT remove salt.

**Removing grease from hands:**
- Use kerosene or petrol (non-polar) rather than water.

**Removing perspiration (sweat) stains:**
- Sweat contains salts and urea (polar).
- Water dissolves it.

### 5.5 Solvent Safety

Non-polar solvents are usually:
- Volatile (evaporate quickly)
- Flammable (fire risk)
- Toxic (harmful vapours)

Always use them in ventilated areas away from flames.

## PART 6: Solubility Calculations

### 6.1 Formula 1 — Mass Solubility

    Solubility (g/100g) = (mass of solute / mass of solvent) x 100

Rearranged for finding mass dissolved:

    Mass of solute = Solubility x (mass of solvent / 100)

### 6.2 Formula 2 — Molar Solubility

    Solubility (mol/dm3) = moles of solute / volume of solution in dm3

### 6.3 Formula 3 — Crystallization on Cooling

    Mass crystallized = m1 - m2
Where:
  m1 = mass dissolved at high temperature
  m2 = mass that can stay dissolved at low temperature

### 6.4 Worked Example 1 — Mass Solubility

**Question:** 25 g of a solute dissolves in 50 g of water at 25 C. Find the solubility in g/100g water.

**Solution:**
  Solubility = (25 / 50) x 100
  Solubility = 50 g/100g water

**Answer: 50 g/100g water**

### 6.5 Worked Example 2 — Molar Solubility

**Question:** 0.2 mol of NaCl dissolves in 500 cm3 of solution. Find the molar solubility. (Na=23, Cl=35.5)

**Solution:**
  V = 500 cm3 = 0.5 dm3
  Solubility = n / V = 0.2 / 0.5 = 0.4 mol/dm3

**Answer: 0.4 mol/dm3**

### 6.6 Worked Example 3 — Crystallization

**Question:** Solubility of KNO3 at 80 C is 170 g/100g water, at 20 C is 30 g/100g water. What mass crystallizes out when 200 g of saturated solution at 80 C is cooled to 20 C?

**Solution:**
  At 80 C: 170 g per 100 g water
  For 200 g water: 340 g dissolved
  At 20 C: 30 g per 100 g water
  For 200 g water: 60 g stays dissolved
  Crystallized = 340 - 60 = 280 g

**Answer: 280 g crystallizes out.**

### 6.7 Worked Example 4 — Find Solubility from Data

**Question:** A saturated solution contains 40 g of solute in 100 g of solution. Find the solubility in g/100g water.

**Solution:**
  Mass of solution = mass of solute + mass of solvent
  If solution = 100 g and solute = 40 g,
  Then solvent = 100 - 40 = 60 g
  Solubility = (40 / 60) x 100 = 66.7 g/100g water

**Answer: 66.7 g/100g water**

**Watch out:** Mass of SOLUTION is not the same as mass of SOLVENT!

## PART 7: Temperature Effects on Solubility

### 7.1 For Solids

Most solids INCREASE solubility with temperature.
Reasons:
- Higher temperature → more kinetic energy
- Solvent molecules more energetic → break solute bonds faster
- Endothermic dissolving process favoured

### 7.2 Exceptions

A few solids DECREASE solubility with temperature:
- Ce2(SO4)3 (cerium sulphate)
- Ca(OH)2 (calcium hydroxide)

### 7.3 For Gases

ALL gases DECREASE solubility with temperature.
Reasons:
- Dissolving gas is exothermic
- Higher temperature favours the reverse (gas escapes)
- Henry's Law: solubility of gas ∝ partial pressure of gas

### 7.4 Pressure Effects on Gases

**Henry's Law:** The solubility of a gas in a liquid is DIRECTLY proportional to the partial pressure of the gas above the liquid.

  S proportional to P

**Everyday example:**
- Soda bottles are sealed under HIGH pressure of CO2.
- When opened, pressure drops → CO2 escapes (fizzing).

## PART 8: 12 Worked Examples (Complete)

### Example 1
**Q:** Solubility of NaCl = 36 g/100g water. Find mass dissolved in 250 g water.
**A:** (36/100) x 250 = 90 g

### Example 2
**Q:** Solubility of KNO3 at 20 C = 30 g/100g. Find mass in 300 g water.
**A:** (30/100) x 300 = 90 g

### Example 3
**Q:** At 60 C, 55 g KCl dissolves in 100 g water. Find solubility.
**A:** 55 g/100g water

### Example 4
**Q:** KNO3 at 70 C = 135 g/100g, at 30 C = 45 g/100g. Find crystallized mass from 100 g water.
**A:** 135 - 45 = 90 g

### Example 5
**Q:** 300 g water saturated at 80 C (solubility 170). Find dissolved mass.
**A:** (170/100) x 300 = 510 g

### Example 6
**Q:** Same solution cooled to 20 C (30 g/100g). Find mass remaining dissolved.
**A:** (30/100) x 300 = 90 g

### Example 7
**Q:** From Example 5 and 6, find crystallized mass.
**A:** 510 - 90 = 420 g

### Example 8
**Q:** 0.15 mol sugar dissolves in 250 cm3. Find molarity.
**A:** 0.15 / 0.25 = 0.6 mol/dm3

### Example 9
**Q:** 20 g solute in 80 g water. Find solubility.
**A:** (20/80) x 100 = 25 g/100g water

### Example 10
**Q:** 60 g solute in 150 g solution. Find solubility.
**A:** water = 150 - 60 = 90 g. Solubility = (60/90) x 100 = 66.7 g/100g

### Example 11
**Q:** Solubility of a gas doubles when pressure doubles. What law applies?
**A:** Henry's Law — gas solubility proportional to pressure.

### Example 12
**Q:** Why does warm soda go flat faster?
**A:** Gas solubility decreases with temperature. Higher temperature → CO2 escapes faster.

## PART 9: JAMB-Style Multiple Choice Questions

### Q1
A solution that cannot dissolve more solute at that temperature is:
A) Unsaturated
B) Saturated
C) Supersaturated
D) Concentrated
**Answer: B** — Saturated means maximum solute at that temperature.

### Q2
Solubility of KNO3 at 40 C is 65 g/100g. Find mass in 200 g water.
A) 65 g
B) 100 g
C) 130 g
D) 200 g
**Answer: C** — (65/100) x 200 = 130 g

### Q3
Which type of mixture is milk?
A) True solution
B) Colloid
C) Suspension
D) Compound
**Answer: B** — Milk is a colloid.

### Q4
Tyndall effect is shown by:
A) Salt solution
B) Sugar solution
C) Colloid or suspension
D) Distilled water
**Answer: C** — True solutions do NOT show Tyndall effect.

### Q5
Solubility of gases as temperature increases:
A) Increases
B) Decreases
C) Unchanged
D) Doubles
**Answer: B** — Gases become LESS soluble at higher temperatures.

### Q6
Which is NOT a colloid?
A) Blood
B) Fog
C) Salt water
D) Milk
**Answer: C** — Salt water is a true solution.

### Q7
To remove oil stains, use:
A) Water
B) Salt water
C) Petrol
D) Vinegar
**Answer: C** — Oil is non-polar; petrol is non-polar (like dissolves like).

### Q8
Solubility of KNO3 at 60 C = 110, at 30 C = 45. Find crystallized mass from 100 g water.
A) 45 g
B) 55 g
C) 65 g
D) 110 g
**Answer: C** — 110 - 45 = 65 g

### Q9
A saturated solution at 80 C (170 g/100g) is cooled to 40 C (65 g/100g). What mass crystallizes out from 50 g water?
A) 52.5 g
B) 65 g
C) 85 g
D) 105 g
**Answer: A** — At 80: (170/100)x50 = 85 g. At 40: (65/100)x50 = 32.5 g. Crystallized = 85 - 32.5 = 52.5 g

### Q10
Harmattan haze is best described as a:
A) True solution
B) Suspension
C) Colloid
D) Compound
**Answer: B** — Harmattan haze is a suspension of dust in air.

## PART 10: Formula Sheet

  Mass solubility (g/100g):   S = (m_solute / m_solvent) x 100
  Mass dissolved:             m = (S / 100) x m_solvent
  Molar solubility:           S = n / V
  Crystallized mass:          m_cryst = m_dissolved_high - m_dissolved_low
  If solution mass is given:  m_solvent = m_solution - m_solute
  Henry's Law (gases):        S proportional to P
  Temperature (K):            K = C + 273
